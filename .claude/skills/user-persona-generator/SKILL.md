# User Persona Generator

Build personas that change product and copy decisions — not decorative posters.

## When to trigger

- "Create personas / target audience profiles / ICP for X"
- Before writing landing pages, onboarding, or pricing
- Structuring raw user-research notes into profiles

## Process

1. **Anchor in reality**: use whatever real signal exists (product description, market, interviews, support tickets). If input is thin, state assumptions explicitly at the top.
2. **2–4 personas max**, differentiated by _behavior and need_, not demographics. If two personas would make the same decisions, merge them.
3. **Per persona**:
   - Name + role + one-line context (make it concrete: "operations supervisor at a 200-person industrial plant", not "manager, 30–45")
   - **Primary goal** — what success looks like in their words
   - **Top 3 pains** — specific, observable frustrations
   - **Current behavior** — what they do today without your product
   - **Objections** — why they'd say no (price, trust, switching cost, compliance)
   - **Buying trigger** — the event that makes them act now
   - **One quote** they'd plausibly say (clearly fictional, illustrative)
4. **Decision hooks**: end each persona with "what this means for us" — 2–3 concrete product/copy implications.

## Rules

- Never present invented personas as research findings; label them "assumption-based — validate with N interviews".
- Avoid stereotype shortcuts (age/gender clichés); differentiate by workflow and constraints.
- For B2B, note the buyer vs. user split explicitly when they differ.

## Quality bar

A teammate reading one persona can predict how that person would react to a pricing page — and be right.
